## 0. Critical transitions / early warning (research focus)

The project's single vertical. A control parameter (`metabolismBase`) is ramped
1 → 40 after a 350-tick plateau; the population collapses to extinction. The
ecology is bistable (low ≈ 40 vs high ≈ 500), and a **no-ramp control survives
8/8** — so the collapse is ramp-caused. Design: 8 seeds × 6 ramp durations
(τ = 300…1050) = 48 runs. Indicators (pooled over 43/48 collapsed runs; AUC,
0.5 = chance):

| indicator | AUC | detect | mean lead (ticks) | mean lead (stress) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| variance | 0.659 | 28% | 207 | 13.9 |
| flicker | 0.562 | 2% | 32 | 2.8 |
| CV | 0.512 | 86% | 160 | 8.6 |
| AR(1) / recovery | 0.493 | 5% | 39 | 1.4 |
| skewness | 0.361 | 7% | 50 | 3.9 |

Findings: **variance** is the workhorse early-warning indicator; flicker is a
distant second; autocorrelation-based indicators are at chance; **skewness is
anti-predictive**. With 8 replicates the previously "non-monotone" AUC-vs-τ curve
resolves into a **monotone decline** — 0.72 → 0.54 as τ grows (slope −0.0003,
R² 0.69) — i.e. warning quality **degrades** the more slowly the tipping point is
approached. The fixed distance-to-bifurcation prediction (**H3**) is **rejected**:
lead in stress units shrinks with τ (CV slope −0.013, R² 0.89). Full charter and
falsifiers: [`SPECIALIZATION.md`](SPECIALIZATION.md).

Reproduce: `npm run research:ews` (from `apps/simulation`; `--quick` to smoke).
Outputs: `output/ews.{md,json,csv}`, `plots/ews.svg`.

### 0.1 Q5 multiverse: does the failure survive the analysis space?

A single specification can always be dismissed as arbitrary, so the registered
analysis was re-run **1,056 ways over the same 48 runs** (4 order parameters ×
3 windows × 8 detrenders × 4 baseline estimators × 3 detection thresholds; any
smoother wider than its window is excluded because it is not a detrender). The
data are held fixed; only the analysis varies. Reproduce with
`npm run research:multiverse` (~15 min) or `-- --from-cache` (~8 s, series
stored in `output/ews-multiverse-series.json`).

The registered specification is inside the space and reproduces the headline
exactly: AUC slope −0.0003 (R² 0.69), lead-in-stress slope −0.0142 (R² 0.34).

| result | registered | multiverse median | 5–95% | share negative |
| :-- | ---: | ---: | :--: | ---: |
| AUC vs τ (variance) | −0.0003 | −0.0001 | −0.0003 to 0.0003 | 73% |
| lead(stress) vs τ (variance) | −0.0142 | −0.0047 | −0.0179 to 0.0047 | 60% |
| lead(stress) vs τ (CV) | −0.0128 | −0.0122 | −0.0169 to 0.0009 | 89% |

Hierarchical-by-seed bootstrap (2,000 resamples, seeds as the independent unit):
AUC slope [−0.0005, 0.0000] (96% negative), lead slope [−0.0203, −0.0120]
(100% negative). So **seed-level uncertainty says the lead shrinks; the AUC
interval reaches zero.**

Three things follow, and the first two cut against the original statement:

1. **The AUC decline is the fragile half.** Its bootstrap interval includes zero,
   and 27% of specifications give a non-negative slope. Substituting log
   population for raw population removes it (median −0.00001, 55% negative); a
   median filter *reverses* it (+0.00032, 0% negative). The claim that "warning
   skill degrades with slower forcing" is therefore specification-dependent in
   AUC units.
2. **The lead-in-stress shrinkage is the robust half.** It is negative under the
   registered analysis, its seed-bootstrap interval excludes zero, and 60% of
   specifications reproduce its sign — but the multiverse median (−0.0047) is a
   third of the registered magnitude, and 40% of analyses give a non-negative
   slope. Once analysis choice is counted, the *magnitude*, and for a large
   minority the *sign*, is uncertain.
3. **No axis level explains it away.** Under the pre-registered rule
   (E-EWS-11: median reduction ≥ 50% **and** ≥ 90% of specifications flattened),
   no level of any axis qualifies. The closest is an adaptive (EWMA) detection
   bar: 100% reduction in the median, 89% of specifications flattened — one
   percentage point below the bar. Bar rigidity is the leading mechanism, not a
   declared one.

**M1 as originally stated is not identifiable here.** The pre-ramp series are
bit-identical across τ for 8/8 seeds (the ramp starts at the end of burn-in and
the baseline window ends before it), so a slower ramp cannot inflate the
pre-ramp baseline. What the baseline axis does test is bar rigidity: a bar whose
scale is fixed while the number of pre-collapse windows grows with τ. That is
the version the EWMA result points at.

Consequence for the thesis: T2 narrows from "warning skill degrades" to **"the
lead in control-parameter units shrinks, the effect is not rescued by plausible
analyses, and its size is specification-dependent."** The mechanism stays open
(Q5), and the honest headline is the one the multiverse produces — a field that
reports one specification overstates how settled a result is.

### 0.2 M4 ablation: is the shrinkage evolutionary rescue?

The first mechanism candidate is **evolutionary rescue (M4)**: a slow ramp gives
lineages more time to adapt, so the population is never as close to the fold as
the schedule says, and the measured lead shrinks. The test moves the capacity to
adapt while holding the ramp schedule fixed, so the thing being measured is
untouched. Three arms, 8 seeds × 6 τ = 48 runs each:

| arm | `mutationScale` | supply (loci/birth) | collapsed | lead slope | seed bootstrap | detection |
| :-- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | :--: | ---: |
| clonal | 0 | 0.0 | 43/48 | −0.01422 | [−0.0181, −0.0088] | 33% |
| registered | 1 | 12.4 | 43/48 | −0.01419 | [−0.0203, −0.0120] | 28% |
| elevated | 4 | 48.3 | 25/48 | n/a | n/a | 8% |

Two checks make this an ablation rather than a correlation. The registered arm is
**re-simulated**, not reused, and reproduces the cached Q5 population series
bit-for-bit (lever check `true`), so adding the lever did not perturb the
baseline. And the mutation supply is **counted** at each birth against the
post-recombination genome — so sexual crossover is not mistaken for mutation —
giving 0 / 12.4 / 48.3 loci per birth (3.90×), exactly the dose the lever was
supposed to deliver. Standing variance is *not* used as the manipulation check:
it is founder-dominated and barely moves between ×0 and ×1 (87% retention in
both), which a 12-run smoke test showed before the full grid ran.

**E-EWS-12: refuted.** The clonal arm retains **100%** of the registered lead
slope (ratio 1.0018), and the two bootstrap intervals nearly coincide. Removing
de-novo mutation and lateral gene transfer does not flatten the lead shrinkage.
The AUC half behaves differently — clonal retains 52% of the registered AUC
slope (−0.0001 vs −0.0003) — so mutation supply accounts for about half of the
*fragile* AUC decline and none of the robust lead decline.

Two results sit beside the verdict and must not be folded into it:

- **Rescue is real.** Quadrupling the supply cut collapse from 43/48 to 25/48,
  two-sided Fisher exact **p = 9.1×10⁻⁵**. Adaptation changes *whether* the
  system collapses.
- **The elevated arm has no slope.** With 8% detection and a zero-resample
  bootstrap, its −0.0182 is fitted from two pre-collapse windows; the runner
  reports `n/a`. A slope is read only above 20% detection and 100 bootstrap
  resamples.

So adaptation is a real process in this ecology, but the mutation supply is not
the mechanism of the lead shrinkage. Selection on the standing variation of the
300 founders and crossover remain untested; M4 as a family is narrowed, not
closed.

Reproduce: `npm run research:ablation` (from `apps/simulation`; ~20 min) or
`-- --from-cache` (seconds). Outputs: `output/ews-ablation.{md,json,csv}`,
`plots/ews-ablation.svg`.

### 0.3 Experiment A: does maximising reproduction breed fragility?

A separate, self-contained model (Life-like CA on a 32×32 torus, heritable
18-bit rule, cells are not individuals). Eight lineage seeds × four arms — no
selection (drift), top 50%, top 25%, top 12.5% — for 20 generations. All four
arms of a seed share a viability-gated Conway-plus-1–3-flips ancestor. The
environment is fixed during evolution; the shock arrives afterwards and suddenly:
per-cell noise, a mortality pulse, or a compute budget. Retention is the
post-shock population over the last 30 of 150 ticks divided by the pre-shock
population, with every shock level starting from the same snapshot.

The first pilot selected on raw 0 → 1 counts and converged on the period-2 global
flipper: 40,960 births = 1,024 cells × 40 ticks, the hard ceiling. Every selected
endpoint was identical, so the design runs **both** a raw-birth endpoint (primary,
the question as asked) and an established-offspring endpoint (newborn cells still
alive 5 ticks later) over the same ancestors.

| endpoint | rho(reproduction gain, retention change) | selected − drift | dose rho (p) |
| :-- | :--: | :--: | :--: |
| raw births, noise | −0.087 [−0.461, 0.286] | +0.018 [−0.028, 0.100] | 0.13 (0.51) |
| established, noise | −0.282 [−0.645, 0.122] | −0.034 [−0.059, −0.010] | −0.19 (0.35) |

All three gates passed (selected lineages gained substantially more held-out
reproduction than drift; 100% of selected endpoints were assayable; effects were
not saturated), and **A-DOOM-01…04 are not supported**. The negative point
estimates do not survive their intervals, and the only interval that excludes
zero — the established selected − drift contrast under noise — is a 3.4-point
retention cost, one third of the pre-registered 0.10 floor, with no dose trend.
Median final retention of selected endpoints is ≈1.0 across all three shocks,
whereas the same assay sends Conway to 0.37 under the mortality pulse: the null
is not an insensitive instrument.

Honest reading: no doomsday in this regime. Selection for reproduction did not
buy fragility at any dose we could apply; at most there is a direction, an order
of magnitude below the assay's noise floor. Larger lattices, longer runs,
multi-species ecologies, or persistent shocks may behave differently, and the
result does not transfer to the agent ecology. Design revisions (the saturated
raw metric, a 32-cell pre-shock floor, and a robust turnover adjustment) are
recorded in [`CLAIMS.md`](CLAIMS.md).

Reproduce: `npm run research:doomsday` (from `apps/simulation`; ~2 min) or
`-- --from-cache` (seconds). Outputs: `output/doomsday.{md,json,csv}`,
`output/plots/doomsday.svg`.

### 0.4 Experiment B: can a hierarchy reproduce while its offspring destroy it?

A second self-contained model, also outside the agent engine: probabilistic
birth–death on a 40×40 torus for 1,200 ticks, 8 fresh seeds (`sa.0`…`sa.7`) × 8
conditions (full 2³ factorial). Scale 1 is cell division into a nearby empty
site. Scale 2 is a persistent **nest**: four same-tag builders initiate it, it
decays when its local builder fraction falls, gives its lineage a birth bonus,
and at full charge releases a propagule carrying the nest's **current** modal
genotype. Builders pay a birth-rate burden; a non-builder mutant of the same tag
keeps the bonus and divides faster, so the nest is destroyed by its own
descendants. Three interventions target distinct links: `cost` penalises fast
local reproduction, `boundary` seals the build trait at the propagule
(germline sequestration), and `signal` lets nests suppress nearby defector
births at a cost to their own charge rate.

The effect floor is 2 × the between-seed SD of the control late propagule rate,
computed after the run: 0.351 propagules/tick. Claims 01–04 were registered
before the run; the pilot seeds (`sb.`, `sc.`, `chk.`) were excluded.

| condition | late population | late births/tick | late builders | late nests | late propagules/tick |
| :-- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| control | 715 | 32.8 | 0.15 | 15.8 | 0.170 |
| cost | 665 | 28.1 | 0.30 | 31.2 | 0.428 |
| boundary | 717 | 32.8 | 0.10 | 9.1 | 0.096 |
| signal | 710 | 32.1 | 0.75 | 91.0 | 0.632 |
| all three | 652 | 26.8 | 0.88 | 100.4 | 0.727 |

**B-SELF-01 supported.** In the control, local births rose from 30.5 to 32.8
per tick (retention 1.078 [1.072, 1.085]) and population from 695 to 715, while
the propagule rate fell from 1.43 to 0.17 (retention 0.113 [0.049, 0.194]) and
the builder fraction from 0.87 to 0.15 (0.161 [0.094, 0.247]); defectors rose
from 0.15 to 0.86. The decoupled state was reached in 6 of 8 runs at median tick
895. One seed (sa.6) kept 0.36 of its early rate, so the collapse is a mean
effect with escapes, not a uniform extinction.

**B-SELF-02a inconclusive, 02b refuted, 02c supported.** Against control, `cost`
adds 0.258 [0.049, 0.508] propagules/tick — a positive effect whose interval
excludes zero, but below the pre-registered 0.351 floor. `boundary` adds −0.074
[−0.202, 0.034]: sealing the build trait did not restore scale-2 reproduction.
`signal` adds 0.462 [0.260, 0.635] and is the only single-link intervention
above the floor.

**B-SELF-03 inconclusive.** The late builder fraction tracks the late propagule
rate strongly (rho 0.868 [0.778, 0.924]), but the local birth rate does not meet
the registered "unrelated" clause (rho −0.527 [−0.709, −0.293], absolute value
above 0.50). Builder supply looks like the gate; the claim that it is the
*exclusive* gate is not established.

**B-SELF-04 refuted.** The three-way interaction is −0.090 [−0.460, 0.269],
i.e. sub-additive. `signal` alone gives 0.632 and all three give 0.727, a
difference the floor cannot resolve.

Honest reading: the headline question is answered **yes in this model** — local
reproduction persists while the hierarchy's own reproduction falls by an order
of magnitude, and the mechanism is the second level being colonised by its own
non-building offspring. But only the inter-scale signal clears the meaningful-
effect floor. The small-structure penalty has a real, smaller effect; germline
sequestration of the cooperative trait does not help; and the combination adds
nothing to the signal alone. The bottleneck claim survived only its first
clause, so "builder supply, not local births, is the gate" is a hypothesis this
run did not isolate. The boundary result is one mechanism in one ecology, not a
general verdict on boundaries.

Reproduce: `npm run research:selfattack` (from `apps/simulation`; ~30 s) or
`-- --from-cache` (seconds). Outputs: `output/self-attack.{md,json,csv}`,
`output/plots/self-attack.svg`.

### 0.5 Experiment C: which directed disturbance kills an emergent colony?

Question: **which small directed perturbation most effectively destroys an
emergent colony — targeted removal of highly replicative cells, removal of
important geometry or connections, a delayed update, or a regional shutdown —
and is a cell's reproductive participation a causal necessity or only a
correlate?** Model: a probabilistic birth–death colony on a 48 × 48 torus,
750 ticks, perturbation at tick 250. Each cell contributes to a colony-level
public good in proportion to local crowding, `(n/4)^2`; the good is well mixed
inside a connected component, decays, and gates birth at a quorum threshold.
Births consume a global nutrient pool; death rises when the good is low and when
a cell is exposed. The model is self-contained in `research/`.

Design: 16 fresh seeds `ck.0`…`ck.15`, 46 runs each, 736 total — 12 conditions
at a 40% lesion (core, rim, participation, density-matched, age-matched,
uniform random, interface seal, matched random edge cuts, three 100-tick
pauses), four dose curves × six doses (5–40%), and a ten-point location sweep.
Every comparison is paired by seed and shares the pre-burn trajectory. The
assay passes its viability gate: 16 controls, 0 destroyed, mean final/pre 0.81
(minimum 0.67).

| arm | destroyed | final / pre | loss vs control |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| control | 0/16 | 0.81 | 0.00 |
| participation (40%) | **9/16** | 0.36 | 0.55 |
| density-matched (40%) | 3/16 | 0.68 | 0.16 |
| random (40%) | 3/16 | 0.66 | 0.17 |
| age-matched (40%) | 0/16 | 0.82 | −0.02 |
| core / rim (40%) | 0/16 / 0/16 | 0.79 / 0.77 | 0.02 / 0.04 |
| seal / random edges | 0/16 / 0/16 | 0.79 / 0.80 | 0.02 / 0.01 |
| delayParticipation | 0/16 | 0.79 | 0.02 |

Four of the five pre-registered claims are supported; one is inconclusive:

- **C-KILL-01 supported.** Participation removal destroys 9/16 vs random 3/16;
  difference **0.38** [0.06, 0.63], above the 0.30 floor. Seven participation
  runs survive, so this is not a universal kill.
- **C-KILL-02 supported.** Participation − age-matched **0.56** [0.31, 0.81];
  participation − density-matched **0.38** [0.19, 0.63]. All three
  density-matched kills are a subset of the nine participation kills.
- **C-KILL-03 inconclusive.** Sealing the core–periphery interface changes loss
  by **0.012** [−0.017, 0.040], far below the 0.173 floor; seal destruction
  0/16. The falsifier did not fire, but connectivity loss alone is not lethal:
  the seal fragments the colony without killing it.
- **C-KILL-04 supported.** A 100-tick pause destroys 0/16 vs permanent removal
  9/16, and 12/16 paused runs end at ≥90% of their paired control final. Only
  4/16 return to 90% of their *own* pre-lesion count, because the controls
  themselves drift to 0.81 of pre; the registered recovery clause uses the
  contemporaneous control, not the absolute baseline.
- **C-KILL-05 supported.** Centre − rim loss **−0.002** [−0.045, 0.040], inside
  ±0.15; the sweep correlation with distance is 0.00 [−0.12, 0.12]. All ten
  compact-lesion locations destroyed 0/16; loss ranged from −0.022 to +0.023.

The dose response has a sharp threshold. At 5–20% no arm destroys a colony; at
30% participation and density-matched each destroy 2/16; at 40% they separate
(9/16 vs 3/16), while age-matched and uniform random remain at 0/16 and 3/16.
At 30% the participation and matched kills are different seeds, so the
specificity is a high-dose phenomenon rather than a smooth gradient.

Honest reading: the map is mostly negative for geometry, interface and pause.
The geometric core, the rim, the core–periphery seal, a temporary freeze and the
lesion's location are not kill switches at this scale. The one positive result
is a **large distributed lesion**, not a small one: removing the 40% most
participatory cells (328 of roughly 820 cells) still leaves 7/16 colonies alive.
Within that limit the effect is not age or density alone — the age-matched arm
destroys none, and the density-matched kills are a strict subset of the
participation kills — but fragmentation is not sufficient either: density-
matched and random lesions fragment almost as much and mostly survive. Five
contrasts on 16 seeds carry no family-wise correction, and the lower bounds of
C-KILL-01 and the density half of C-KILL-02 are only one and three paired runs
above zero. The result is one ecology, not a general law of participation.

Reproduce: `npm run research:killswitch` (from `apps/simulation`; ~5 min) or
`-- --from-cache` (seconds). Outputs: `output/kill-switch.{md,json,csv}`,
`output/plots/kill-switch.svg`; pre-registration and verdicts in

### 0.6 Experiment D: can early structure predict collapse on unseen conditions?

Question: **can an emergent colony's collapse be predicted from ticks 40–140,
before it is visible, on mutation rules and parameter regimes the model has not
seen — and are the resulting probabilities calibrated?** The experiment runs the
agent engine unchanged. Design: 3 mutation rules (`mutationScale` 0, 1, 4) × 8
`(metabolismBase, foodRegenRate)` regimes = 24 conditions, 8 fresh seeds each,
192 runs of 600 ticks. Training is clonal/legacy × P0,P1,P2,P6,P7 (10
conditions, seeds `d.c0`…`d.c7`); test is elevated × all 8 regimes (unseen rule)
plus clonal/legacy × P3,P4,P5 (unseen parameters), 14 conditions, seeds
`d.c8`…`d.c15`. Features are 14 population, temporal, entropy, spatial-structure
and energy statistics computed only in the early window. The model is an
L2 logistic regression (`λ = 0.1`) standardised on the training split; baselines
and ablations use the same specification and training runs.

The label gate passed: 62 labelled training runs (39 collapse / 23 persist, 18
ambiguous) and 92 labelled test runs (59 collapse / 33 persist, 20 ambiguous).
Collapse is late mean alive < 10, persistence ≥ 30, and the 10–30 interval is
excluded.

| model | features | test AUC | 95% cluster CI | Brier skill | ECE | slope |
| :-- | ---: | ---: | :--: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| full | 14 | **0.885** | [0.825, 0.941] | −0.060 | 0.250 | 379,686 |
| population (level + trend) | 2 | 0.712 | [0.593, 0.805] | 0.043 | 0.149 | 355,302 |
| temporal | 6 | 0.775 | [0.659, 0.857] | 0.141 | 0.145 | 0.495 |
| entropy | 2 | 0.812 | [0.733, 0.881] | 0.225 | 0.104 | 2.093 |
| structure | 5 | 0.627 | [0.526, 0.730] | −0.304 | 0.268 | 250,789 |
| full − entropy | 12 | 0.885 | [0.826, 0.940] | −0.058 | 0.247 | 436,373 |
| full − structure | 9 | 0.713 | [0.589, 0.810] | −0.160 | 0.250 | 478,308 |

Two claims are supported, one is inconclusive, two are refuted:

- **D-PRED-01 supported.** The full model ranks held-out collapse above
  persistence at AUC **0.885** [0.825, 0.941]. The features end 360 ticks before
  the outcome window opens.
- **D-PRED-02 inconclusive.** The AUC gain over the population baseline is
  **0.173** [0.103, 0.288], but Brier skill is **−0.060**, missing the
  registered 0.10 bar. The AUC clause passes; the probability clause does not.
- **D-PRED-03 refuted.** ECE **0.250** and calibration slope **379,686**:
  predictions pile near 0 and 1. The 0.8–1.0 bin contains 77 of 92 runs, predicts
  0.993 and observes 72.7% collapse; the 0.2–0.4 bin predicts 0.293 and observes
  0/4. Brier is 0.244 against a 0.230 base-rate score.
- **D-PRED-04 refuted.** Entropy alone reaches AUC **0.812** [0.733, 0.881], so
  the registered "entropy fails the bar" null is false in this grid. The carrier
  is spatial entropy (species entropy alone is at chance, AUC 0.500); entropy is
  also redundant in the full model, which stays at 0.885 without it.
- **D-PRED-05 supported.** Full minus temporal is **0.110** [0.046, 0.238]:
  structure adds ranking information beyond population dynamics. It is not a
  standalone detector — structure-only AUC is 0.627 — and removing structure
  from the full model drops AUC to 0.713.

Honest reading: **the ranking transports, the probabilities do not.** Early
population and spatial structure contain real information about which unseen
condition will collapse, and the full model beats every baseline on AUC. But the
same model is badly overconfident out of condition, with negative Brier skill
and a calibration slope far outside any usable range. The registered design
separated these two questions, and they returned different answers. The
entropy-only result is a refutation of a pre-registered null, not evidence that
entropy is a universal collapse signal; species diversity alone carries no
signal here, and the full model does not need entropy once structure and
temporal features are present.

Limits: 14 held-out conditions, one engine, 600 ticks, one unseen mutation scale,
absolute alive-count labels, 38/192 ambiguous runs excluded, and feature
families that are correlated enough that the ablations are predictive contrasts,
not causal effects. The bootstrap resamples conditions and treats unseen-rule
and unseen-parameter conditions as exchangeable. No intervention or rescue is
tested.

Reproduce: `npm run research:collapse` (from `apps/simulation`; ~8 min) or
`-- --from-cache` (seconds). Outputs: `output/collapse-predict.{md,json,csv}`
and the append-only cache `output/collapse-predict-cache.jsonl`;
pre-registration and verdicts in [`CLAIMS.md`](CLAIMS.md) §6.

radar H0–H10, life‑organization O0–O9, emergence H1–H3, macro regime, and rigor
a–d. The consolidated statement of every finding — with its process traces
(`P-LOOP`, `P-RECIP`) and a cross‑reference matrix — is
`research/UNIFIED_RECORD.md`.

  ledger.
