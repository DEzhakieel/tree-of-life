## 0. Critical transitions / early warning (research focus)

The project's single vertical. A control parameter (`metabolismBase`) is ramped
1 → 40 after a 350-tick plateau; the population collapses to extinction. The
ecology is bistable (low ≈ 40 vs high ≈ 500), and a **no-ramp control survives
8/8** — so the collapse is ramp-caused. Design: 8 seeds × 6 ramp durations
(τ = 300…1050) = 48 runs. Indicators (pooled over 43/48 collapsed runs; AUC,
0.5 = chance):

| indicator | AUC | detect | mean lead (ticks) | mean lead (stress) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| variance | 0.659 | 28% | 207 | 13.9 |
| flicker | 0.562 | 2% | 32 | 2.8 |
| CV | 0.512 | 86% | 160 | 8.6 |
| AR(1) / recovery | 0.493 | 5% | 39 | 1.4 |
| skewness | 0.361 | 7% | 50 | 3.9 |

Findings: **variance** is the workhorse early-warning indicator; flicker is a
distant second; autocorrelation-based indicators are at chance; **skewness is
anti-predictive**. With 8 replicates the previously "non-monotone" AUC-vs-τ curve
resolves into a **monotone decline** — 0.72 → 0.54 as τ grows (slope −0.0003,
R² 0.69) — i.e. warning quality **degrades** the more slowly the tipping point is
approached. The fixed distance-to-bifurcation prediction (**H3**) is **rejected**:
lead in stress units shrinks with τ (CV slope −0.013, R² 0.89). Full charter and
falsifiers: [`SPECIALIZATION.md`](SPECIALIZATION.md).

Reproduce: `npm run research:ews` (from `apps/simulation`; `--quick` to smoke).
Outputs: `output/ews.{md,json,csv}`, `plots/ews.svg`.

### 0.1 Q5 multiverse: does the failure survive the analysis space?

A single specification can always be dismissed as arbitrary, so the registered
analysis was re-run **1,056 ways over the same 48 runs** (4 order parameters ×
3 windows × 8 detrenders × 4 baseline estimators × 3 detection thresholds; any
smoother wider than its window is excluded because it is not a detrender). The
data are held fixed; only the analysis varies. Reproduce with
`npm run research:multiverse` (~15 min) or `-- --from-cache` (~8 s, series
stored in `output/ews-multiverse-series.json`).

The registered specification is inside the space and reproduces the headline
exactly: AUC slope −0.0003 (R² 0.69), lead-in-stress slope −0.0142 (R² 0.34).

| result | registered | multiverse median | 5–95% | share negative |
| :-- | ---: | ---: | :--: | ---: |
| AUC vs τ (variance) | −0.0003 | −0.0001 | −0.0003 to 0.0003 | 73% |
| lead(stress) vs τ (variance) | −0.0142 | −0.0047 | −0.0179 to 0.0047 | 60% |
| lead(stress) vs τ (CV) | −0.0128 | −0.0122 | −0.0169 to 0.0009 | 89% |

Hierarchical-by-seed bootstrap (2,000 resamples, seeds as the independent unit):
AUC slope [−0.0005, 0.0000] (96% negative), lead slope [−0.0203, −0.0120]
(100% negative). So **seed-level uncertainty says the lead shrinks; the AUC
interval reaches zero.**

Three things follow, and the first two cut against the original statement:

1. **The AUC decline is the fragile half.** Its bootstrap interval includes zero,
   and 27% of specifications give a non-negative slope. Substituting log
   population for raw population removes it (median −0.00001, 55% negative); a
   median filter *reverses* it (+0.00032, 0% negative). The claim that "warning
   skill degrades with slower forcing" is therefore specification-dependent in
   AUC units.
2. **The lead-in-stress shrinkage is the robust half.** It is negative under the
   registered analysis, its seed-bootstrap interval excludes zero, and 60% of
   specifications reproduce its sign — but the multiverse median (−0.0047) is a
   third of the registered magnitude, and 40% of analyses give a non-negative
   slope. Once analysis choice is counted, the *magnitude*, and for a large
   minority the *sign*, is uncertain.
3. **No axis level explains it away.** Under the pre-registered rule
   (E-EWS-11: median reduction ≥ 50% **and** ≥ 90% of specifications flattened),
   no level of any axis qualifies. The closest is an adaptive (EWMA) detection
   bar: 100% reduction in the median, 89% of specifications flattened — one
   percentage point below the bar. Bar rigidity is the leading mechanism, not a
   declared one.

**M1 as originally stated is not identifiable here.** The pre-ramp series are
bit-identical across τ for 8/8 seeds (the ramp starts at the end of burn-in and
the baseline window ends before it), so a slower ramp cannot inflate the
pre-ramp baseline. What the baseline axis does test is bar rigidity: a bar whose
scale is fixed while the number of pre-collapse windows grows with τ. That is
the version the EWMA result points at.

Consequence for the thesis: T2 narrows from "warning skill degrades" to **"the
lead in control-parameter units shrinks, the effect is not rescued by plausible
analyses, and its size is specification-dependent."** The mechanism stays open
(Q5), and the honest headline is the one the multiverse produces — a field that
reports one specification overstates how settled a result is.

### 0.2 M4 ablation: is the shrinkage evolutionary rescue?

The first mechanism candidate is **evolutionary rescue (M4)**: a slow ramp gives
lineages more time to adapt, so the population is never as close to the fold as
the schedule says, and the measured lead shrinks. The test moves the capacity to
adapt while holding the ramp schedule fixed, so the thing being measured is
untouched. Three arms, 8 seeds × 6 τ = 48 runs each:

| arm | `mutationScale` | supply (loci/birth) | collapsed | lead slope | seed bootstrap | detection |
| :-- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | :--: | ---: |
| clonal | 0 | 0.0 | 43/48 | −0.01422 | [−0.0181, −0.0088] | 33% |
| registered | 1 | 12.4 | 43/48 | −0.01419 | [−0.0203, −0.0120] | 28% |
| elevated | 4 | 48.3 | 25/48 | n/a | n/a | 8% |

Two checks make this an ablation rather than a correlation. The registered arm is
**re-simulated**, not reused, and reproduces the cached Q5 population series
bit-for-bit (lever check `true`), so adding the lever did not perturb the
baseline. And the mutation supply is **counted** at each birth against the
post-recombination genome — so sexual crossover is not mistaken for mutation —
giving 0 / 12.4 / 48.3 loci per birth (3.90×), exactly the dose the lever was
supposed to deliver. Standing variance is *not* used as the manipulation check:
it is founder-dominated and barely moves between ×0 and ×1 (87% retention in
both), which a 12-run smoke test showed before the full grid ran.

**E-EWS-12: refuted.** The clonal arm retains **100%** of the registered lead
slope (ratio 1.0018), and the two bootstrap intervals nearly coincide. Removing
de-novo mutation and lateral gene transfer does not flatten the lead shrinkage.
The AUC half behaves differently — clonal retains 52% of the registered AUC
slope (−0.0001 vs −0.0003) — so mutation supply accounts for about half of the
*fragile* AUC decline and none of the robust lead decline.

Two results sit beside the verdict and must not be folded into it:

- **Rescue is real.** Quadrupling the supply cut collapse from 43/48 to 25/48,
  two-sided Fisher exact **p = 9.1×10⁻⁵**. Adaptation changes *whether* the
  system collapses.
- **The elevated arm has no slope.** With 8% detection and a zero-resample
  bootstrap, its −0.0182 is fitted from two pre-collapse windows; the runner
  reports `n/a`. A slope is read only above 20% detection and 100 bootstrap
  resamples.

So adaptation is a real process in this ecology, but the mutation supply is not
the mechanism of the lead shrinkage. Selection on the standing variation of the
300 founders and crossover remain untested; M4 as a family is narrowed, not
closed.

Reproduce: `npm run research:ablation` (from `apps/simulation`; ~20 min) or
`-- --from-cache` (seconds). Outputs: `output/ews-ablation.{md,json,csv}`,
`plots/ews-ablation.svg`.

### 0.3 Experiment A: does maximising reproduction breed fragility?

A separate, self-contained model (Life-like CA on a 32×32 torus, heritable
18-bit rule, cells are not individuals). Eight lineage seeds × four arms — no
selection (drift), top 50%, top 25%, top 12.5% — for 20 generations. All four
arms of a seed share a viability-gated Conway-plus-1–3-flips ancestor. The
environment is fixed during evolution; the shock arrives afterwards and suddenly:
per-cell noise, a mortality pulse, or a compute budget. Retention is the
post-shock population over the last 30 of 150 ticks divided by the pre-shock
population, with every shock level starting from the same snapshot.

The first pilot selected on raw 0 → 1 counts and converged on the period-2 global
flipper: 40,960 births = 1,024 cells × 40 ticks, the hard ceiling. Every selected
endpoint was identical, so the design runs **both** a raw-birth endpoint (primary,
the question as asked) and an established-offspring endpoint (newborn cells still
alive 5 ticks later) over the same ancestors.

| endpoint | rho(reproduction gain, retention change) | selected − drift | dose rho (p) |
| :-- | :--: | :--: | :--: |
| raw births, noise | −0.087 [−0.461, 0.286] | +0.018 [−0.028, 0.100] | 0.13 (0.51) |
| established, noise | −0.282 [−0.645, 0.122] | −0.034 [−0.059, −0.010] | −0.19 (0.35) |

All three gates passed (selected lineages gained substantially more held-out
reproduction than drift; 100% of selected endpoints were assayable; effects were
not saturated), and **A-DOOM-01…04 are not supported**. The negative point
estimates do not survive their intervals, and the only interval that excludes
zero — the established selected − drift contrast under noise — is a 3.4-point
retention cost, one third of the pre-registered 0.10 floor, with no dose trend.
Median final retention of selected endpoints is ≈1.0 across all three shocks,
whereas the same assay sends Conway to 0.37 under the mortality pulse: the null
is not an insensitive instrument.

Honest reading: no doomsday in this regime. Selection for reproduction did not
buy fragility at any dose we could apply; at most there is a direction, an order
of magnitude below the assay's noise floor. Larger lattices, longer runs,
multi-species ecologies, or persistent shocks may behave differently, and the
result does not transfer to the agent ecology. Design revisions (the saturated
raw metric, a 32-cell pre-shock floor, and a robust turnover adjustment) are
recorded in [`CLAIMS.md`](CLAIMS.md).

Reproduce: `npm run research:doomsday` (from `apps/simulation`; ~2 min) or
`-- --from-cache` (seconds). Outputs: `output/doomsday.{md,json,csv}`,
`output/plots/doomsday.svg`.
