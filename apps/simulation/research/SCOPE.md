## Purpose

To determine whether a seeded, fully deterministic artificial-life engine reproduces the canonical phenomena of complex-systems science — and to state, before each experiment, what result would refute that determination. The engine is the instrument; the benchmark suite, the known-systems registry and the rigor layer are the calibration, validation and error-control procedure around it.

## Method commitments

- **Determinism discipline** (M-DET) — Every experiment is re-run and compared by full-state hash. This establishes bit-reproducibility; it says nothing about whether the modelled behaviour is correct.
- **Verification against closed forms** (M-VER) — Where the reference model has an analytic solution, the engine is checked against it. This establishes internal correctness; it says nothing about correspondence to nature.
- **Validation against published measurements** (M-VAL) — Where a source reports a number, the engine is scored against that number with the source’s own uncertainty. Establishes correspondence for the specific quantities tested only.
- **Statistical error control** (M-SIG) — One-sided tests with Holm–Bonferroni FWER control and Bayes factors. This controls false positives within a declared family; it does not make an effect large or important.
- **Interventional ablation** (M-ABL) — Each claimed mechanism is switched off and the predicted effect direction checked. This supports a causal reading within the model; it does not transfer causality to the real system.
- **Parsimony control** (M-PAR) — Competing configurations are compared with AIC/BIC, charging 2k / k·ln n for free parameters. A configuration that fits only by retuning more knobs is penalised, so support cannot be purchased with complexity.
- **Language models as proposers, not judges** (M-AI) — LLM output is cached, deduplicated and clamped to whitelisted knobs; every verdict is harness-derived. This bounds the model’s authority but does not make its proposals meaningful on their own.
